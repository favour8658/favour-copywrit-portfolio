Campaign concept · Starbucks Pike Place® Roast

Make room for your morning.

The brief

Make a coffee pause feel like a small moment of calm in a busy morning.

Featured copy

Before the messages. Before the rush. Before your day starts asking for everything. Take a moment that’s yours. Make room for your morning.

Read the concept, extra headlines & social copy

Campaign idea

The morning can ask a lot of you before you’ve had a moment to think. This concept treats coffee as an invitation to pause—not to make the day perfect, but to start it on your own terms.

Headlines

  • Make room for your morning.
  • Before the day gets loud, find your quiet.
  • A little pause can change the pace.

Social post 1

Before the messages. Before the rush. Before your day starts asking for everything. Take a moment that’s yours. Make room for your morning.

Social post 2

Your morning doesn’t have to be perfect. Just make a little room for yourself in it. ☕

Creative thinking

The concept focuses on the feeling behind a coffee ritual: a moment of calm before the day gets busy. The headline can lead a campaign, while the social copy adapts the idea for everyday moments.

Email concept · Coursera welcome series

Your next big idea can start small.

The brief

Welcome a new learner and make exploring a course feel manageable.

You don’t need a perfect plan to start learning something new. You just need a place to begin. Pick a topic that interests you, explore at your own pace, and see where your curiosity leads.

Email 1 · Welcome

Subject: Your next big idea can start small

Hi there,

You don’t need a perfect plan to start learning something new. You just need a place to begin.

Maybe there’s a skill you’ve been meaning to explore, a question you want to answer, or a subject you’ve always found interesting. Start there. Take a look around, find a course that catches your attention, and see where your curiosity leads.

Learning doesn’t have to happen all at once. One lesson, one new idea, one small step—that’s a start worth celebrating.

Here’s to staying curious,
The Coursera team

Email 2 · Gentle follow-up

Subject: Still curious about that topic?

Preview text: Pick up an idea whenever you’re ready.

Hi there,

Keep following your curiosity,
The Coursera team

Creative thinking

The first email welcomes; the follow-up gives the reader a low-commitment way to continue. The sequence encourages action without relying on pressure.

Nigerian campaign concept · Indomie

No long story. Just Indomie.

The brief

Create a playful campaign for busy moments when someone wants a quick, familiar meal.

Featured copy

Lecture don finish. Traffic don happen. Hunger don show. No long story—make your Indomie, take a breath, and enjoy your moment.

Social line: Busy day? No long story. Just Indomie.

Read the full campaign concept & social ideas

Campaign idea

Some days come with a full schedule and very little room between one thing and the next. You get home from lectures, finish a long shift, or finally sit down after a day of errands—and suddenly, hunger becomes the next item on the list.

This concept is built around the familiarity of that moment. It uses a light, conversational voice to make the meal feel easy to choose and easy to enjoy. “No long story” is a friendly way to get straight to the point: when you want a quick, familiar bowl, there’s no need to overthink it.

The campaign can speak to students, busy households, and anyone looking for a simple meal break. The language can flex between English and everyday Nigerian Pidgin, while keeping the message clear and inclusive. The aim is not to make assumptions about every Nigerian’s routine, but to capture one recognizable kind of busy-day moment.

Social post 1

Lecture don finish. Traffic don happen. Hunger don show. No long story—make your Indomie, take a breath, and enjoy your moment.

Social post 2

That “I need something quick” moment? We know it. No long story. Just Indomie.

Social post 3

One bowl. A little break. Back to your day when you’re ready.

Creative thinking

The idea uses conversational Nigerian phrasing to create a familiar, light-hearted voice. It keeps the product benefit simple and avoids unverified claims about nutrition, preparation time, or ingredients. The short campaign line is flexible enough to work across social posts and other campaign formats.
